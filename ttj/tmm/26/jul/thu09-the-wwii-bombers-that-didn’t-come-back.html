The WWII bombers that didn't come back

In the Second World War, the US military had a problem.

Too many bombers were getting shot down over Europe, and they wanted to add armour. But armour's pretty heavy, you just can't cover the whole plane and expect it to fly the same…

So where do you put it?

SURVIVORSHIP BIAS

Simple, they thought, we'll just look at the planes coming back, map out where the bullet holes are, and stick armour on those spots. 🤷

And so they started gathering data on where all the bullet holes were…

Holes all over the wings, the fuselage, the tail.

A pretty clear pattern…

So that's where the armour should go, right?

Not so fast…

A mathematician named Abraham Wald looked at the same data and said...

“No, you've got it totally wrong”

Here's what he actually saw…

Those planes being mapped were the ones that made it home.

The holes in the wings and fuselage?

Those are the places a bomber can get hit and still fly.

The engines and the cockpit had barely a scratch on the returning planes…

Not because they didn't get hit there, but because the planes that did get hit there never came back…

The armour needed to go where there were no holes.

Wald was reading the damage on the survivors and working out, from the gaps, where the planes that got shot down must have been hit.

Now.

Here's why this matters for traders…

This has a name…it's called survivorship bias.

Take your blotter or journal, for example…

Every trade in it is a trade you took… so you go through it looking for what works and what doesn't, sensible stuff.

But your blotter is basically the planes that came back…

There's a whole other set of trades that never made it, the setups you passed on.

Shot down if you like before they ever reached the data, and it's possible that you're not analysing a single one.

Here's what makes it interesting…

The trades you didn't take can fall into two buckets.

#1 Some were good setups you skipped. Maybe you hesitated, leaving your edge on the table.

#2 Some were crap trades you correctly avoided. Your filter's working…

It's the same missing data but with opposite lessons.

And you can't really tell which one you are... until you look.

So here's something to consider doing...

  • Start recording the trades you don't take.
  • Screenshot the setup, mark where your stop and target would've gone, then check back later and see what happened.

Review them like real trades.

Most traders analyse the trades they took… not many look at the ones they didn't.

And that missing half of your blotter might be the most valuable data you've got.

Might be worth a try…
